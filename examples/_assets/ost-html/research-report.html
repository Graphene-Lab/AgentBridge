1. Executive Summary

This report summarizes the simplest ways for a small bakery to take orders online for the period September 2026. It was prepared by AgentBridge Research Team at the request of the bakery owner and is based on a comparison of three options: a third-party marketplace, an own simple shop, and a full e-commerce platform.

Overall, the simplest ways for a small bakery to take orders online are a marketplace or an own simple shop, and the own simple shop offers the best long-term value. The key findings are set out in Section 3 and the resulting recommendations in Section 6; the recommended approach is to start with a simple own shop and add click-and-collect for lower fees and a direct customer relationship.

2. Background

The bakery currently takes orders in person and by phone. Customers increasingly expect to order online, and the owner wants to add an online ordering channel without a large investment of time or money. Three options were considered: Option A — Marketplace, Option B — Own simple shop, and a full platform.

The objective of this report is to compare the options on setup effort, cost, fees and customer ownership, and to recommend the simplest route that protects long-term value for a small bakery.

3. Findings

The following findings were identified during the review:

  1. Option A — Marketplace is fast to start, but fees are high and the bakery does not own the customer relationship.
  2. Option B — Own simple shop is a small branded store with more control, moderate setup effort and the best long-term value.
  3. The first-year cost estimate for the own shop is EUR 3,100, compared with EUR 4,200 for the marketplace and EUR 9,800 for the full platform.
  4. Adding click-and-collect to an own shop lowers fees and strengthens the direct customer relationship.

4. Analysis

The three options were assessed on setup effort, ongoing fees, control and customer ownership. Option A — Marketplace requires the least setup effort but charges high fees and keeps the customer relationship with the marketplace. Option B — Own simple shop requires moderate setup effort but gives the bakery a small branded store with more control. A full platform offers the most features but at EUR 9,800 in the first year is disproportionate for a small bakery.

First-year cost estimate: Marketplace EUR 4,200; Own shop EUR 3,100; Full platform EUR 9,800. The own shop is therefore EUR 1,100 cheaper than the marketplace in the first year and EUR 6,700 cheaper than the full platform.

Beyond cost, the decisive factor is customer ownership. An own shop keeps customer data and order history with the bakery, which supports repeat orders and direct marketing. Adding click-and-collect reduces delivery costs and fees while matching how local bakery customers prefer to buy. The marketplace remains a useful secondary channel for discovery, but it should not be the primary ordering channel.

5. Conclusions
  • The simplest ways for a small bakery to take orders online are a marketplace and an own simple shop; both can be launched quickly.
  • The own simple shop costs less in the first year (EUR 3,100) than the marketplace (EUR 4,200) and far less than the full platform (EUR 9,800).
  • An own shop with click-and-collect gives lower fees and a direct customer relationship, which is the best long-term value.
6. Recommendations
  1. Start with a simple own shop as the primary online ordering channel.
  2. Add click-and-collect to lower fees and build a direct customer relationship.
  3. Keep a marketplace listing only as a secondary channel for discovery, and monitor its fees.
  4. Defer the full platform until order volume and revenue justify the EUR 9,800 first-year cost.

High-priority recommendations (start with a simple own shop; add click-and-collect) should be implemented by December 2026; the remaining recommendations are expected to be completed by June 2027.
