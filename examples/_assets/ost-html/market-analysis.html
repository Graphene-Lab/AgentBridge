1. Executive Summary

This report summarizes the market analysis of the specialty coffee retail segment — size, trends, competition and outlook — for the period 2024–2026. It was prepared by AgentBridge Research Team at the request of the management board and is based on AgentBridge market data, published industry figures and comparative analysis of the competitive landscape.

Overall, the specialty coffee retail market remains in a solid growth phase, expanding from EUR 1.6 billion in 2024 to EUR 2.1 billion in 2026, with subscription models, single-origin beans and online ordering acting as the strongest growth drivers; a few large chains dominate the segment, but independent shops win on quality and local loyalty, and the outlook is favourable for small players who pair quality products with a strong online presence. The key findings are set out in Section 3 and the resulting recommendations in Section 6; the recommendations focus on e-commerce capability, subscription offers, sourcing transparency and local differentiation.

2. Background

The specialty coffee retail segment has moved from a niche positioning to a mainstream category of the coffee market. Growth has been supported by rising consumer interest in product quality, origin traceability and brewing at home. At the same time, digital channels have changed how customers discover, order and reorder coffee, and competition between large chains and independent shops has intensified.

Against this background, this report sets out to quantify the size of the specialty coffee retail market for 2026, to identify the trends that are driving growth, to compare the positioning of large chains and independent shops, and to assess the outlook for small players. The findings are intended to inform assortment, channel and investment decisions for the coming planning period.

3. Findings

The following findings were identified during the review:

  1. The specialty coffee retail market reached EUR 2.1 billion in 2026, up from EUR 1.85 billion in 2025 and EUR 1.6 billion in 2024.
  2. Subscription models, single-origin beans and online ordering are the strongest growth drivers in the segment.
  3. A few large chains dominate the segment, while independent shops win on quality and local loyalty.
  4. The outlook is favourable for small players who pair quality products with a strong online presence.

4. Analysis

The market has grown consistently over the review period. Measured against EUR 1.6 billion in 2024, the segment grew to EUR 1.85 billion in 2025 and to EUR 2.1 billion in 2026 — an increase of approximately 31% over two years. Growth is therefore not a short-term effect but a structural expansion of the category.

On the demand side, three drivers stand out. Subscription models convert occasional buyers into recurring customers and stabilise revenue. Single-origin beans support higher price points and attract quality-oriented consumers. Online ordering lowers the barrier to trial and repeat purchase and extends reach beyond the catchment area of a physical shop. Together, these drivers explain the largest part of the observed growth.

On the supply side, a few large chains dominate the segment through scale, distribution and brand visibility. Independent shops compete successfully on quality and local loyalty, which are difficult for chains to replicate at scale. The interpretation is that the segment is not a winner-takes-all market: small players can hold and grow profitable positions provided they combine product quality with a strong online presence, which is precisely where the growth drivers and their own strengths overlap.

5. Conclusions
  • The specialty coffee retail market is growing steadily and reached EUR 2.1 billion in 2026.
  • Subscription models, single-origin beans and online ordering are the strongest growth drivers and should be treated as core rather than optional channels.
  • Although a few large chains dominate, the outlook is favourable for small players who pair quality products with a strong online presence.
6. Recommendations
  1. Introduce or expand a coffee subscription model to secure recurring revenue and improve customer retention.
  2. Strengthen the single-origin assortment and communicate origin, roast and quality attributes at the point of sale and online.
  3. Invest in online ordering and e-commerce capability, including delivery and click-and-collect options.
  4. Differentiate on quality and local loyalty rather than competing with large chains on price and scale.

High-priority recommendations (1, 2) should be implemented by Q1 2027; the remaining recommendations are expected to be completed by Q4 2027.
